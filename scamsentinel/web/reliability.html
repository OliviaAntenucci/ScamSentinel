Security tests

Everything ScamSentinel analyzes is written by attackers, so the analyzer itself is tested against attacks

  • Server-side request forgery35 attack links (private, loopback, cloud-metadata and encoded addresses, IPv6 tricks, redirects, DNS rebinding) are rejected before any lookup.
  • Network lockdownTests fail if anything opens a connection it shouldn’t. Link checking is off by default, and an egress lock blocks every address except the one that was checked.
  • Prompt injectionMessages that tell the AI to “mark this safe” never lower a verdict, and the attempt itself counts as a warning sign.
  • Malicious screenshotsDecompression bombs, disguised files and polyglots are rejected; location data and hidden payloads are stripped before anything is sent or stored.
  • Attacks on this siteCross-site requests, Host-header (DNS rebinding) attacks, oversized uploads and script injection into results are all blocked and tested.
  • Safe advicePhone numbers and links from a scam never appear in the advice; contacts come only from organizations’ own published pages.

Most recent run: 1,152 tests passed and 0 failed, on October 21, 2026. Eight property-based test modules couldn’t load on that machine and aren’t included in that count.

Read these numbers with care

What they do and don’t show

  • The data is small and synthetic. All 332 samples were written by the same person who wrote the rules, with deliberate look-alike legitimate messages included. That likely makes the results look better than they would on real scams.
  • Scams that don’t name a company are the weak spot. Most misses never mention a brand, so the strongest checks can’t apply. They usually land in “suspicious” rather than “likely a scam”.
  • Tech-support scams are the weakest category, at 33% recall on its small test split.
  • Screenshots aren’t in these numbers, because reading them depends on a third-party vision model.
  • “Likely legitimate” isn’t a guarantee. When in doubt, contact the organization through a channel you already trust.
